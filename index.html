Why I Started MIH

I never lacked ideas. I lacked a system for finishing.

Over my career I've worked across nearly every creative discipline there is: photography, music, graphic design, web, content, copywriting, video, talent sourcing, event production. I never struggled to know what to make. The what was always right in front of me.

What I struggled with was the why, and the how long. I'd get partway into building something, decide I wasn't ready, and go learn the skill again instead of finishing what I'd started. The vision would get cloudy, not because I lacked ability, but because I never built the system that carries a vision from a good idea to something real.

MIH is that system. It's how I hold myself, and now my clients, to seeing a project through from origin to delivery: not just launched, but sustained.

That's the work I do for you. I take your vision, turn it into something people can actually experience, and build the momentum that keeps it going long after launch.

The Problem

Uncertainty doesn't announce itself. It shows up everywhere else.

It shows up in proposals that undersell the real value of the work. In a team that describes the company differently depending on who's asking. In partners and investors who walk away unsure, not because the offering was weak, but because the message never landed.

The first move is usually a new logo, a new website, a new tagline. Those can help. But they don't fix anything if the story behind them was never settled. A new website built on an unclear message is still an unclear website. Design can sharpen a brand. It can't decide who that brand is for you.

What uncertainty actually costs you:

  • Your team explains the organization differently depending on who's in the room
  • Your website, marketing, and customer experience tell different stories instead of one
  • Growth depends on you personally explaining the vision, again and again, instead of the brand doing that work on its own
  • People understand what you do, but hesitate because they don't understand why it matters
  • Every new marketing push starts from zero, with nothing to build on

This isn't a talent problem. It isn't a marketing problem. It's a clarity problem, and until it's resolved, every logo, campaign, and website is working harder than it should have to.

Introducing The Clarity Shift

A framework, not a fix.

The Clarity Shift is how I resolve who an organization is, make that resolution felt, and build the systems that keep it that way. It moves in order: three deliberate stages, each one built on the last.

Jump straight to a new look before the story underneath it is settled, and you get something attractive but hollow: confident design wrapped around a story you haven't actually agreed on yet.

I started MIH to turn organizational uncertainty into intentional human experience.

The Three Pillars

Vision, Experience, Momentum.

01

Vision: See Who You Actually Are

Vision resolves identity: purpose, positioning, and one working narrative the whole organization can repeat the same way. I sit down with leadership and frontline teams, take an honest look at how the organization currently describes itself, and build one story that reconciles the differences.

Skip this stage and a rebrand changes nothing. The surface shifts. The disagreement about identity stays put and resurfaces within the year.

02

Experience: Make It Felt, Not Just Said

Experience takes the resolved narrative and turns it into what people actually encounter: the website, the sales conversation, the physical space, the tone of an email. The goal is one clear impression, not five well-designed but disconnected ones.

Most people want to start here. I put it second on purpose. An experience built without a resolved vision behind it tends to be polished, well-produced, and forgettable.

03

Momentum: Make It Stick

Momentum keeps the clarity intact after the work is done: playbooks, templates, and operating rhythms that let you carry the vision and experience forward without leaning on me, or anyone else, to keep explaining it.

Most consulting relationships skip this stage. That's why so much strategy work quietly fades within eighteen months. Momentum is the difference between a project and a system.

Vision without Experience is a deck nobody reads. Experience without Vision is a beautiful surface with nothing underneath. Neither survives without Momentum.
